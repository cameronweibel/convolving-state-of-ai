The State of AI in Mid-Market Investment Management: Q1 2026

Adoption, automation, and the road to enterprise-scale AI across PE, asset management, and family offices.

April 2026

This report is for informational purposes only and does not constitute investment advice. Convolving is not a licensed or qualified investment advisor.

More than 80% of mid-market investment firms say they are now using AI in at least one function, yet fewer than one in five have moved beyond experimentation to scale it across their operations.

Key findings

  1. Adoption is widespread, but depth remains shallow. 83% of mid-market investment firms report using AI in at least one business function, up from 61% two years ago. However, only 17% have progressed beyond piloting.
  2. Deal sourcing and due diligence lead adoption. Investment teams most commonly deploy AI for deal flow screening (68%) and due diligence acceleration (57%), while portfolio monitoring and LP reporting lag behind.
  3. Agentic AI is on the radar but not yet in production. 54% of respondents say they are experimenting with AI agents, but only 8% have deployed them in a live workflow. The most common use case is automated research synthesis.
  4. Firms investing in workflow redesign see 3x the impact. Organisations that redesigned processes around AI (rather than layering it on top) report three times higher satisfaction and measurable time savings of 30%+ on target tasks.
  5. Talent, not technology, is the primary bottleneck. 62% cite internal skills gaps as the top barrier to scaling AI, ahead of data quality (48%) and compliance concerns (41%).
  6. Budget allocation is rising but fragmented. Average AI spend among mid-market firms increased 45% year-over-year, though most lack a centralised AI budget, with spending scattered across IT, operations, and individual deal teams.

AI adoption continues to broaden, but most firms remain in early stages

Our survey of 420 investment professionals across private equity, asset management, and family office firms shows that AI usage has become the norm. The share of firms reporting regular AI use has climbed steadily since 2021, accelerating sharply after the arrival of generative AI tools in late 2022.

Yet at the enterprise level, the majority are still experimenting. Only 17% of respondents say their firm has moved into the scaling or fully integrated phase.

The gap between "using AI" and "getting value from AI" is where most firms are stuck. We see the same pattern repeatedly: individual team members adopt ChatGPT or Copilot for ad-hoc tasks, the firm counts that as "AI adoption," but no workflow has actually changed. The firms that break through are the ones willing to redesign how work gets done, not just add a new tool to the existing process.

Deal sourcing and due diligence lead AI adoption by function

When we look at where firms are deploying AI, deal sourcing and due diligence stand out as the most mature use cases. These functions benefit from large volumes of unstructured data (company profiles, news, filings) that AI can process at scale. Portfolio monitoring and LP reporting, despite being data-heavy, remain comparatively under-served.

Agentic AI is generating excitement, but production deployments remain rare

AI agents that can plan, execute multi-step tasks, and interact with external tools represent the next frontier. More than half of respondents say their firm is at least exploring agentic capabilities. However, only 8% report having an agent in a live production workflow, reflecting the significant infrastructure and governance requirements involved.

Early adopters report meaningful operational impact

While enterprise-wide financial impact remains limited, firms that have moved beyond experimentation report tangible benefits at the use-case level. Time savings in due diligence and deal screening are the most commonly cited, followed by improved consistency in reporting and reduced manual data entry.

The firms seeing real ROI are not the ones with the fanciest tools. They are the ones that started with a specific, measurable workflow bottleneck, built a focused solution, and iterated. A well-designed AI workflow for CIM summarisation can save an analyst 4 to 6 hours per deal. Multiply that across a deal team running 50 processes a year and the impact becomes substantial.

Talent and process, not technology, are the primary barriers to scaling

When asked about the biggest obstacles to scaling AI, respondents overwhelmingly point to people and process challenges rather than technology limitations. Internal skills gaps lead the list, followed by data quality concerns and regulatory uncertainty.

AI budgets are growing but remain fragmented across teams

Average AI-related spending among mid-market investment firms rose 45% year-over-year. Yet the majority of firms still lack a centralised AI budget. Spending is typically scattered across IT, deal teams, and operations, making it difficult to track ROI or coordinate initiatives firm-wide.

Looking ahead: from experimentation to transformation

Mid-market investment firms stand at an inflection point. The tools are available, the awareness is high, and early adopters are demonstrating real returns. But bridging the gap between pockets of experimentation and firm-wide transformation requires deliberate investment in three areas: workflow redesign, talent development, and governance frameworks.

The next twelve months will separate the firms that treat AI as a strategic capability from those that treat it as a set of tools. We expect to see the first wave of mid-market firms achieve genuine workflow transformation, particularly in due diligence and portfolio monitoring. The key question is not whether to adopt AI, but how quickly firms can move from individual experimentation to coordinated, process-level integration.
